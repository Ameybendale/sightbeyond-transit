Hear which bus is yours.

SightBeyond Transit helps blind and low-vision passengers ride public buses on their own. Small Bluetooth beacons at stops and on buses talk to a phone app, and the app speaks: where you are, which bus is arriving, and when to get off.

Beacons broadcast who they are

iBeacons sit at bus stops and inside buses and send out a short advertisement packet at regular intervals. Each packet holds a 128-bit UUID, a 16-bit Major value (stop or route category) and a 16-bit Minor value (the specific stop or bus).

The phone listens and measures

The app scans for nearby BLE signals and reads the signal strength (RSSI). A stronger reading means the beacon is closer, and the strongest one is selected as the nearest.

Noise is filtered, IDs are matched

Walls, vehicles and crowds make RSSI jump around, so the app applies signal averaging and threshold filtering. The stable beacon ID is then matched against stored route and stop data, locally or in the cloud.

Text-to-Speech says what to do

The matched context becomes a spoken message such as "Bus number 121 is coming" or "Merry Weather Station is coming, be ready to get off the bus". The interface uses large buttons and optional voice commands.

Technology and system modules

Bluetooth Low Energy

2.4 GHz, low power, and beacons are detected passively in advertising mode.

iBeacon protocol

Structured UUID, Major and Minor IDs map exact places to digital information.

RSSI

Signal strength estimates distance, smoothed with averaging and thresholds.

Text-to-Speech

Turns instructions into clear audio, the main way the user interacts.

Android app

Runs on any Bluetooth LE phone with 2 GB of RAM or more.

Route database

Local or cloud storage for bus routes and stops, so it works without live internet.

Four modules

Beacon Detection

Scans, measures RSSI and finds the strongest nearby beacon.

Data Processing

Matches IDs to routes and filters out irrelevant signals.

Audio Guidance

Converts navigation instructions to spoken alerts.

Mobile Interface

Simple screens built for minimal interaction.

Limits and what's next

Current limits

Needs a Bluetooth smartphone. Works only where beacons are installed, placement must be accurate, and buildings or crowds affect signal strength. Outdoor and bus-level only, with no indoor station navigation.

Future enhancements

AI-based RSSI filtering, real-time bus tracking, multilingual voice, vibration feedback, solar-powered beacons and smart city integration.

Applications

Independent daily travel, help at bus stops, guidance on board, and a way to make public transport more inclusive without major infrastructure changes.
